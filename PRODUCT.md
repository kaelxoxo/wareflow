# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Operations managers, warehouse supervisors, and inventory administrators at small-to-mid-size businesses. They work at desks and on warehouse floors, switching between tracking stock levels, recording movements, and investigating discrepancies. Their context is task-driven: they arrive with a specific job (add a shipment, check a low-stock alert, find a SKU) and want to complete it fast and move on. Secondary users are owners who review KPI dashboards and manage team access, and viewers who reference inventory data without editing it.

## Product Purpose

Wareflow is a multi-tenant warehouse inventory management tool. It lets teams track SKUs across multiple warehouses, record inbound and outbound stock movements, manage role-based access, and monitor inventory health via a live dashboard. Success looks like: any team member with the right role can accomplish their task in under 60 seconds without hunting for options or reading documentation.

## Brand Personality

Clean, approachable, capable. Wareflow should feel like a well-made professional tool — the kind of app that gets out of the way and lets you work. Warm enough not to feel sterile, confident enough not to need decoration. Reference energy: Notion (friendly clarity, never condescending, respects the user's intelligence).

## Anti-references

- **Bloated enterprise**: Heavy sidebars, deeply nested menus, overwhelming tables with 20+ columns, modal-within-modal flows, settings that require a consultant. Wareflow should never feel like SAP or legacy ERP.
- **Over-animated startup**: Skeleton loaders on every panel, entrance animations on routine interactions, transitions that make the app feel like a product demo rather than a working tool. Motion should be purposeful and rare.

## Design Principles

1. **Task first**: Every screen is oriented around a single primary action. Navigation and secondary options recede; the work comes forward.
2. **Trusted, not flashy**: Motion, color, and decoration are used to communicate state (success, warning, empty) — not to impress. The app should feel stable and reliable, not trendy.
3. **Appropriate density**: Operations users read tables and lists, not cards. Prefer table layouts for data. Use whitespace for rhythm, not emptiness.
4. **Role-aware clarity**: Admins and owners see more controls; managers and viewers see a simpler surface. The UI should never show a disabled button where a hidden one would do.
5. **Approachable expertise**: New users can orient themselves without a walkthrough. Power users find depth without it being imposed on everyone.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Keyboard-navigable forms and modals. Color is never the only indicator of state (always pair with text or icon). Support for dark mode (already implemented via CSS class toggle).
